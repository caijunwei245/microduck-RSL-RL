## The one large move: velocity walk 48 % -> 88 % — and it is NOT the latency envelope

Controlled: the same checkpoint and the same five seeds under the pre-fix envelope give

| envelope | velocity walk |
|---|---|
| measured latency (1-2 held) | 22/25 (88 %) |
| pre-fix (3-6 dithered) | **21/25 (84 %)** |

So the envelope is worth nothing measurable on this row (84 vs 88 at n=25 is inside the ±13-point
interval), and the move away from 48 % is **era**: the 48 % figure is from 2026-09-23, before the
sustained-forward bucket (+8 points, measured that day), before the turn/wobble work, and at 3 seeds
instead of 5. The rehearsal's "collapses to 0.00 m/s at delay 4-6" (which motivated the envelope
change) describes the *single-episode* rehearsal, not this 25-round gate. Worth keeping straight,
because the obvious story ("we fixed the latency, walking jumped") is wrong.

## The latency default is a net win with a measured COST on the skating rows

The two rows that came in below their record were re-gated at the same 5 seeds under the pre-fix
envelope (`MICRODUCK_ACTUATOR_LAG="3,6" MICRODUCK_ACTUATOR_LAG_HOLD=0`) — seeds matched, so the
comparison is paired by construction:

| row | new envelope (1-2 held) | old envelope (3-6 dithered) |
|---|---|---|
| roller_crouch | 19/25 (76 %) | **25/25 (100 %)** |
| roller_standup | 22/25 (88 %) | **25/25 (100 %)** |

**RESOLVED 2026-09-28: it was a train/test mismatch, and retraining removes it.** Both families got an
arm under the measured envelope (same recipe and warm-start source, only the envelope differs,
`MICRODUCK_WARM_START=1`; `logs/roller_lag12_arms.sh`), and both come back to perfect:

| row | old ckpt, old envelope | old ckpt, new envelope | **retrained arm, new envelope** |
|---|---|---|---|
| roller_crouch | 25/25 (100 %) | 19/25 (76 %) | **25/25 (100 %)**, median z_max 138.9 mm |
| roller_standup | 25/25 (100 %) | 22/25 (88 %) | **25/25 (100 %)**, median z_max 143.0 mm |

The retrained roller_standup is also qualitatively better in the rotation: `upright_frac` 0.97-1.00
against 0.61-1.00 for the old checkpoint, i.e. it now HOLDS the stand instead of spending a third of
the episode tilted.

Together with the two controls above (walk 88 vs 84, recovery 0.95-0.99 either way), the envelope's
measured effects are therefore: **no pass-rate cost at all once each family is trained under it, no
measurable change on walk or recovery, and two benefits that are not pass rates — fidelity (1-2 steps
is what the hardware does) and reproducibility (first-step `ctrl` spread 5.4e-03 -> 1.5e-06).** The
turn row's improvement was the wobble-tax fix, not this. The rows in `skill_demo.py` now point at the
retrained roller arms.

Both roller rows are perfect under the envelope they were TRAINED with and lose 12-24 points under the
new one. Walking and turning move the other way (walk 48 -> 88 %, turn tracking gain 0.68 -> 1.2), and
the walk rehearsal is explicit that the deployment candidate collapses at delay 4-6 (0.00 m/s) while it
walks at 0.26-0.27 at delay 1-2. So the honest statement is:

* **the latency envelope is task-dependent**, and evaluating an old checkpoint under a new envelope is
  a train/test mismatch, not a property of the recipe;
* the roller families therefore need their own arm trained under the measured envelope (a ~1-2 k
  iteration warm start each) before any of these numbers is treated as the recipe's result. Until
  then, quote roller rows with the envelope they were measured under, and prefer the pre-fix envelope
  when the question is "did this roller policy regress?".

| standup, floor (tilt >= 60 deg) | 0.953 sustained 0.953 (n=256) | 0.973 sustained 0.973 (n=256) |

Two conclusions. (a) The envelope is **ruled out** as an explanation for the recorded 0.812 — both

correction still stands: the 0.996 figure it replaced was the env's spawn mix, not the pin.

## The ball_kick row was measuring the wrong axis (found while probing the misses)

`logs/kick_contact_probe.py` was written to separate "the foot never reaches the ball" from "it arrives
too gently". It found neither: **every round strikes the ball at control step 5-8 and sends it off at
~0.4-0.5 m/s**. What varied was direction — and that is when the bug surfaced: the row scored the
ball's **world-x** displacement, while `reset_ball_in_front_of_foot` places the ball in the **robot's
yaw frame** and the reward projects onto that direction, with the spawn yaw randomised. One "clean
miss" (`ball +0.000 m`, the round that made the rotation read 4/5) actually sent the ball **+1.181 m
straight ahead** at a yaw where world x barely moves. Re-measured in the robot's frame:

| | old criterion (world x) | fixed criterion (robot frame) |
|---|---|---|
| ball_kick right, 5 seeds x 5 rounds | PARTIAL 13/25 (52 %) | **PASS 25/25 (100 %)** |
| per round | 0.6-1.0 m, one 0.000 | **1.15-1.40 m every round** |

It also explains the result that made this row look like a physics problem: reducing the ball's spawn
noise to 0.5x and 0.25x changed nothing (52/48/48 %) — of course it did not, the failures were in the
scoring. **So the contact-gate arm planned for this row is dropped: there is nothing to fix.**

## Rows that did not move, and should therefore be treated as the real gaps
